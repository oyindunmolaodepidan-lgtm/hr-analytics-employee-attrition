# Employee Attrition & HR Analytics Dashboard

## Project Overview

This project analyzes employee attrition and workforce trends using Power BI.

The goal of the project is to understand employee turnover and identify patterns across different employee characteristics, departments, job roles, income bands, overtime status, age groups, gender, and tenure.

The analysis provides insights that can help organizations better understand employee retention and identify areas that may require attention.

---

## Business Objective

The main objective of this project is to analyze employee attrition and answer questions such as:

- What is the overall employee attrition rate?
- Which departments have the highest attrition?
- Does overtime relate to higher employee attrition?
- Which income bands have the highest attrition?
- Which job roles experience higher attrition?
- How does attrition vary across age groups?
- How does employee tenure relate to attrition?
- Are there differences in attrition between male and female employees?

## Dashboard Analysis

### Attrition by Department

The dashboard shows differences in attrition across departments.

- Sales recorded the highest attrition at approximately 7.1%.
- Human Resources followed at approximately 6.9%.
- Finance recorded approximately 6.7%.
- Research & Development recorded approximately 5.2%.
- IT recorded approximately 4.5%.
- Marketing recorded approximately 4.1%.
- Operations recorded the lowest attrition at approximately 2.8%.

### Attrition by Overtime

Employees working overtime recorded a higher attrition rate than employees who did not work overtime.

- Overtime: approximately 8.4%
- No Overtime: approximately 3.3%

This suggests that overtime may be an important area to investigate when assessing employee retention.

### Attrition by Income Band

Employees in the lower income bands recorded higher attrition rates.

- Low income: approximately 9.9%
- Low-Middle income: approximately 5.2%
- High income: approximately 3.5%
- Upper-Middle income: approximately 2.4%

This indicates that compensation and income level may be an important factor to consider when investigating employee turnover.

### Attrition by Tenure

Employees with shorter tenure recorded higher attrition.

- 0–2 years: approximately 9.2%
- 3–5 years: approximately 5.8%
- 11+ years: approximately 4.6%
- 6–10 years: approximately 2.4%

The results highlight the importance of employee retention strategies, particularly for employees in their early years with the organization.

### Attrition by Age Group

The dashboard shows differences in attrition across age groups.

- 45–54 years: approximately 6.2%
- 55+ years: approximately 5.8%
- 25–34 years: approximately 4.8%
- 18–24 years: approximately 4.8%
- 35–44 years: approximately 4.2%

### Attrition by Gender

- Male employees: approximately 5.7%
- Female employees: approximately 4.5%

### Attrition by Job Role

Attrition varied considerably across job roles.

Some of the higher attrition rates were observed among:

- Laboratory Technicians: approximately 7.5%
- Research Scientists: approximately 7.3%
- Managers: approximately 6.6%
- Sales Executives: approximately 6.3%

Lower attrition was observed among roles such as:

- Data Analysts: approximately 2.4%
- Manufacturing Directors: approximately 2.8%
- Finance Analysts: approximately 3.4%

## Key Insights

1. The overall employee attrition rate is approximately **5.1%**, with 77 employees lost out of 1,500.

2. Employees working overtime have a noticeably higher attrition rate than employees who do not work overtime.

3. Employees in the lowest income band have the highest attrition rate, suggesting that compensation may be an area worth investigating.

4. Employees with 0–2 years of tenure have the highest attrition rate, indicating that early-stage employee retention may require additional attention.

5. Attrition varies significantly across departments, with Sales recording the highest rate and Operations recording the lowest.

6. Attrition also differs considerably across job roles, with Laboratory Technicians and Research Scientists among the roles with higher attrition.

7. The dashboard shows differences in attrition across age groups and gender, providing additional areas for workforce analysis.

---

## Recommendations

Based on the analysis, the following actions could be considered:

### 1. Strengthen Early Employee Retention

Organization should consider improving onboarding, mentorship, training, and employee engagement initiatives for employees within their first two years.

### 2. Review Overtime Practices

Since employees working overtime show a higher attrition rate, management should review workload distribution and overtime practices to reduce potential employee burnout.

### 3. Review Compensation

The higher attrition among employees in the lower income band suggests that compensation and career progression should be reviewed for employees in these groups.

### 4. Focus on High-Attrition Departments

Departments such as Sales and Human Resources should be investigated further to understand the factors contributing to their higher attrition rates.

### 5. Investigate High-Risk Job Roles

Roles with relatively high attrition should be examined to identify potential issues involving workload, compensation, career progression, management, or job satisfaction.

### 6. Improve Employee Engagement

Organizations can use employee satisfaction and engagement data to identify areas where employee experience can be improved.
